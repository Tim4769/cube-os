# New linkage simulation and pathing

Instead of the pushing mechanism of the last linkage I want to design another pathing in which the movement will be controlled more by the stepping forward and pulling for the foot

- Simulated and tinkered with the 4-bar linkage calculator until I get the pathing that looks good

![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.10.24.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwMTcsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--89251207e4f4360a9b052dde0d4a648f0339e5bf/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.10.24.png)

Using the same proportion and the same logic as before but halving the size from the indicated numbers on screen I get this:

![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.10.44.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwMTgsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--664b6963b605b8ce6d196132a1d3575985575696/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.10.44.png)

The pathing simulated is the same as the one in the calculator, so I added the back foot part

![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.11.06.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwMTksInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--96224f07aa06d7f806ea0032be7af248cebe8f11/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.11.06.png)

The motion looks much more like walking than beforeI also extended the length of the foot proportionally to the length of the leg to expect more stability

I then added an approximation of the main body and mirrors another leg so I can have an idea of how it looks compared to the last version

![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.11.26.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwMjAsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--ff2e476d40a35abb058499b4ae3b01ce3736c1fe/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.11.26.png)

Proportionally it looks way better than before. For stability, I also added another side of the foot so there is more surface area in contact with the ground so it hopefully does not fall when one foot is lifted

![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.11.53.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwMjIsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--89b4bf0f6c1af4525cfb8cda575c6772ed39da04/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.11.53.png)

To connect the two sides of the foot together, I initially wanted to connect it at both ends of the foot but realised that it will collide with the linkages as the robot walks forward and backwards. Instead, I created two holes in the middle of the foot that does not interfere with the linkage and use threaded holes on both sides of the foot so I can later on connect them with screws without nuts or inserts.

![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.12.11.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwMjMsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--51eeb93e2a76d5fd3f670f2f24563c3410c3d4e9/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.12.11.png)

I then design a fixer just for the testing phase so it can fix the fixed bar in the front portion of the foot as well as the back portion

![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.12.29.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwMjQsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--7fa8c0d30626c8f895cbde1ee099df630d94e081/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 21.12.29.png)

Just to make sure everything fits nicely, I also designed a test block to test whether the threaded holes I made can hold the M3 screws we had and printed it. The result looks good so I continued to print the testing leg portions with the H2D printer using basic PLA as it seems to be strong enough.
