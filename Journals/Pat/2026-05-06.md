# Assembling and testing linkage

I assembled the 3D printed 4-bar linkage using screws spacers and nuts → I had to align the bars so that nothing collides with each other when the crank bar turns anti clockwise

- I will take this into account when designing the new leg version
- I also need to figure out how to attach the linkage to the motor

![Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 21.17.32.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTIxODUsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--9e37092d9dc31111414280d7e766cefb82def4b7/Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 21.17.32.png)

I realised that the foot part is too thick to be assembled with screws so I decreased the thickness of the foot and printed it again

I also needed to change the configuration of the bars so that the ground bar is furthest back, so that it can be easily attached to the main body while allowing the crank bar to easily be attached and driven by the motor

## Designing the mock up fixer

This can also give an idea of how to actually fix the leg to the main body

I designed a mockup fixer bar to test out the linkage but the first design has a protruding section right next to the crank joint which interferes with the rotation of the crank

![Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 10.06.19.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTIxODYsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--3740e77f04df37d48afba1521567ae5482734da0/Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 10.06.19.png)

I then changed the fixer to be longer with the elevating extrusion to be on the other side of the elevated part. I also designed fixer so that the further left and right bar can be fixed on the inside part of the fixer → I can possibly use this design for the final leg.

![Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 21.18.37.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTIxODcsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--3bd63e53539ec9510d46b24c864816e9b4520415/Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 21.18.37.png)

After printing the fixer, I tested out the fixer with the new configuration of the bar → it looks like it would work but the foot will have to be on both side of the linkage so that linkage can hold up the main body by itself. 

![Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 21.21.16.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTIxOTMsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--3825aac0c51d9d6411fa38a4d1ec62d07f00cc42/Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 21.21.16.png)

![Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 21.21.59.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTIxOTQsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--ba96ae2467fc8bc81ea4e8707df4fdd77c2b50d1/Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 21.21.59.png)

- The elevating extrusion can also be closer to the screw hole for the small bar for the back foot as it only rotates lower than 90 degrees clockwise and anticlockwise
- I also tested the strength of configuration by dropping it from a table height and I tried throwing it down with more force as well as there was no way to yet simulate the load of the body no bars snapped or screw came lose so the linkage can be quite good in terms of strength
