# Assembling and testing v2 linkage

I retrieved the printed parts from the printer and I tried assembling the linkage.

- What worked fine:
    - I first tested out **the connection between the two parts of the feet** which works perfectly fine with the threads holding well the two parts of the legs
    - the **front part linkage of the foot** also works fine with the extra extrusion from the linkage bars to give space for the head of the screw working fine → maybe for further using other heads other than counterbore can decrease the space needed hence decreasing the thickness of the leg
- Problems:
    - The **back foot linkage** encounter the problem where the head of the counterbore screw gets stuck on the testing fixer because the thickness of the screw is three mm but the space is only 2 mm
        - I addressed this by extending the width of the space in the hole of the fixer where the linkage will be hosted, and also increased the spacer at the small bar of the back part to 3.5 mm instead of 2 mm
    - I also realised that I did not model the threads in the connection between the two linkages and the outside part of the foot
        - I just replaced the 3.5 mm diameter hole with the threaded hole for M3 screws instead

I then reprinted the parts that needed editing and assembled it again

![Screenshot 2026-05-09 at 22.55.45.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTM0MzYsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--5d4aec8fcc364fdd410d0215d5ab0fdccbc359b3/Screenshot 2026-05-09 at 22.55.45.png)

Everything looks to be working properly with the leg being able to stand by itself with the two side of the feet 

Things to improve:

- I have noticed that **the joint between the rear short bar and rear long bar can flip** towards the front which would make it stuck on the crank joint which would prevent the crank bar from being rotated further
    - This can be fixed by moving the fixing point of the rear linkage backwards or create a flap to prevent the joint from flipping → the former might be simpler
- The **two feet** part also cannot fit in very tightly with the linkages because of the head of the screw that fixes the two linkages with the outside part of the foot
    - For this I can just extend the cylinder that links the two to hide the screw and make the hole cut for the head of the screw on the outside part of the foot deeper, so that a short screw can still hold the foot together

Next steps:

- Edit the parts that need editing and print the mirrored part
- Join the two legs together with a cube that mimics the main body of the CubeOS
- Design the mount for the motor so it can actually drive this linkage
