# Leg mechanism brainstorming

**Objective**: to brainstorm and design an early prototype that would be the base for the walking mechanism of the desk companion robot

**Requirement**: only driven my one motor on each side and should best fit the mood of the robot while maintaining some kind of mobility

## Linkages

Got recommended to check out linkages from the slack so I checked some out through these websites:

https://www.csuohio.edu/sites/default/files/47A-2016.pdf

https://www.diywalkers.com/walker-abcs.html

Advantage

- Rotate continuously clockwise → moving in a predetermined path
- Need to know the length of connection and joint position to simulate the path
- Energy efficient
- Can change the number of linkages to fit desired motion
- Move forward and backwards

Disadvantage

- I am not sure how to move length and right
- Might not fit the vibe of the robot

### 4-bar linkage

- Sturdier and more efficient than higher bar counts
- Higher footpath → better for navigating different terrains and obstacles → avoid jamming
- Lower footpath → sturdier and better for larger walkers
- Reducing number of legs → need to increase the time the foot is in contact with the ground
    - Increasing velocity during the lifting can result in force

Jansen linkage calculator: https://www.diywalkers.com/strandbeest-optimizer-for-lego.html

4-bar linkage calculator: https://www.diywalkers.com/4-bar-walking-linkage.html

**Idea**: the leg can be a 4-bar linkage connected with another fixed joint on the frame of the robot to make a foot

- This was chosen due to its higher simplicity → more solid due to lower amount of connections but still maintain the ability to be customisable

I simulated the 4-bar linkage in the 4-bar linkage calculator, messing around the length and angle parameter until getting the foot pathing I would like → this will become the connection of the front of the foot later on

Prototype this on Fusion 360 + the other joint to try it out → connect using screws and spacers for joints → allows free rotation

- B2-B4 joint needs to be fixed but the rest needs to be able to move → try this dimension and testing it with the revolving joints and rigid components where necessary

CADed the simulated 4-bar linkage in Fusion 360 with revolute joints which works according to the simulation

Then added another 2 bar part which is joined with the tip of this 4-bar linkage to create the portion of the back of the foot → added a large slot as a mock up foot or testing

- The total length of the two bars was chosen so that the foot was flat at the highest point of the 4-bar linkage’s rotation allowing it to rest stably
