# Leg design + testing

## Modifications to the original leg design + other mockups

Started with editing the original design as we realised that the original foot as the highest point of the front of the foot will be in the same line as the back the foot will be flat, and will never push down against the ground which is necessary to give the force needed to push the robot forward

![Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 22.46.12.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTE3NjEsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--d7cdcaf2f1d5d17274c10f96e9b2e24ab55f87f1/Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 22.46.12.png)

I decreased the length of the long bar of the back foot so at the highest point of the the front oot there is a slight angle down, allowing the foot to push the robot forward.

I also created a block to mock up the base of the robot so I can fix the frame to it as well as the short bar for the back of the foot for testing.

I then mirrored the foot to the other side for the other leg just for the feeling of the final model and how it should look like → I realized that the legs were way too long and will need to be decreased proportionally in the final design

![Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 22.46.35.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTE3NjIsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--b718402f808b43928f251a5e499b89e91de4d1a7/Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 22.46.35.png)

## Simulation of joint relationship

I also used the animate joint relationship to turn the crank joint to simulate the motor spinning the crank joint → I realized that this configuration of the 4-bar linkage can follow 2 possible foot paths which depends on the starting configuration of the leg

- After couple of rotations which is inconsistent in the Fusion 360 simulation it will turn from one foot path to another
- This also could be because solids can phase through each other in the simulation which will not happen in real life
- I decided that this should be resolved with the physical mockup and testing so I left it as it is

# Main body

Make a main body → decided it will have the dimension of 80*80*80 mm as the PCB that I have talked with Tim will have the dimension of 60*60 which will allow each wall to have 5 mm thickness and 5 mm space in each side for any extra wiring.

![Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 22.48.58.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTE3NjgsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--dd16e29e6223cf0fe36bd03506db4c74970abd76/Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 22.48.58.png)

![Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 22.49.16.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTE3NjksInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--d80ea5b78b0e705acf1a07f428ddfabde0af2e06/Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 22.49.16.png)

- The bottom is thicker with 15 mm to hold the motor
- The back plate is held to the main body with 4*M3 screws I added threaded holes so the screws can directly be screwed into the print without inserts
- All edges of the cubes except the bottom two on the side are fileted → the two on the bottom side are not fileted as they will be covered by the legs and motor
