# CubeOS robot design sync — mechanical, electrical, software

# Summary

Tim and Pat discussed the first concrete design direction for CubeOS: a compact cube-shaped interactive robot with screens, environmental sensors, microphone/speaker, camera, and servo-driven legs. The meeting centered on defining the product concept, narrowing mechanical assumptions, identifying electronics constraints, and creating a near-term prototype plan.

## Product Concept

- Cube-like robot form factor, initially estimated around 5 × 5 × 5 cm, potentially closer to ~7 cm once frame, screens, and mechanical clearance are included.
- Interactive remote communication device: one robot can send voice/messages to another robot, making it feel more physical and social than a phone chat.
- Potential study-buddy mode using camera presence to make remote studying feel less lonely.
- Possible movement mirroring feature: movement or gesture detected on one side could be represented on the paired robot or screen.

## Hardware / Electronics Notes

- Planned electronics include: ESP32-S3, 1.54 in TFT screens, USB-C charging/data, battery management, gyro/IMU, ambient light, IR/temperature, humidity, CO2, microphone, speaker, and camera.
- Pin budget is tight once camera and screens are included; software/electrical planning must account for ESP32-S3 GPIO limits.
- Air-exposed sensors need enclosure ventilation. Proposed solution: small holes in the frame/bottom so humidity/CO2/environment sensors can contact room air without visually large openings.
- Component sizing and datasheets are needed before finalizing frame and motor-holder geometry.

## Mechanical Notes

- Locomotion is expected to use two small servo motors, likely mounted outside or partly integrated into the lower body because there is insufficient internal cube volume.
- Main mechanical risks: leg collapse, slipping, insufficient torque, oversized motors, and awkward proportions if motors dominate the body.
- Feet likely need rubber or high-friction contact surfaces.
- Leg geometry should be prototyped before committing to full enclosure design.
- A simple flat-sheet test platform with motor holders was preferred as the fastest V1 test rig.
- MG90S-style micro servos were discussed as a likely candidate, pending final dimension/torque check.

## Software / Interaction Notes

- Core communication feature: remote message/voice transmission between paired robots.
- Later software features may include AI-mediated messages, camera-based presence/study buddy mode, sensor-based room condition reporting, and motion/gesture representation.
- Software scope is acknowledged as non-trivial and should follow after mechanical proof-of-concept and pin planning.

## Decisions

- Continue with a cube-like body rather than a non-cube form unless screen/frame constraints force a change.
- Prioritize leg mechanics as the first prototype because it is the highest-risk subsystem.
- Use a flat-sheet or simplified lower-body prototype before a complete cube enclosure.
- Use small frame ventilation holes for environmental sensors rather than a large visible opening.
- Organize the project inside the existing CubeOS Notion workspace using department pages plus the current task, roadmap, meeting-notes, and decision databases.

## Open Questions

- What exact servo model provides enough torque while fitting the desired proportions?
- Should servos be fully external, partially embedded, or hidden in a lower body/pants-like section?
- What maximum leg rotation angle works before tipping or scraping becomes unacceptable?
- How many screens are final, and how will they share SPI/control lines?
- How much of voice, AI chat, camera study-buddy, and movement sync fits into the first software milestone?
