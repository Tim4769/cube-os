This is the first time I am doing impedance-controlled differential pairs

Used the impedance calculator from JLCPBC https://jlcpcb.com/pcb-impedance-calculator

![Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 12.29.13 PM.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTE5NDUsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--50655f642c312f3fd8482bc7a68d83275388bf06/Screenshot 2026-05-05 at 12.29.13 PM.png)

The differential pair is done, but since the USB-C connector has duplicated pins to plug in both ways, it was difficult to do length matching for the DN1 DN2 and DP1 DP2.

When I was downloading 3 models from websites, Snap Magic showed me a white box when I clicked the download button and didn’t start the download. I ended up using Ultraliberian instead of Snap Magic for some of the 3d models.

I’ve decided to place the buck from the battery voltage to 3.3V on the corner of the PCB, far away from the main components like the ESP32 camera, etc., to reduce the interference from the inductor switching.

The space in front of the camera cable is left empty for easier assembly of the camera after the PCB arrives.

The differential pairs of the USB-C have their own nets and are colored in yellow to allow easy identification when routing, so that they can be prioritized first. Other high-speed signals, like the screen and camera, are colored pink for similar purposes.

![Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 8.57.26 AM.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTE5NDQsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--7058b754354b1f397e058bdc51b32a06e13389bc/Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 8.57.26 AM.png)

A four-layer PCB is chosen due to the advantages of signal integrity from GND planes and easier routing on the top and bottom layers

![Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 8.57.14 AM.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTE5NDMsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--1d7ddc8380fd2df9e7ff24a238de14105a80ef53/Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 8.57.14 AM.png)

4 mounting holes that are M2 are used to ensure the stability of the PCB in the cube.

![Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 9.00.18 AM.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTE5NDIsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--3e221732361cb4e77b115423fb720e2f3b6344b6/Screenshot 2026-05-06 at 9.00.18 AM.png)
The back screen is removed because it is not going to be used frequently, and it consumes more energy and pins on the ESP32. With the removal, one extra pin from the ESP32 is free, allowing me to add features like a button for quicker manipulation of the cube's settings without needing to go to the website to edit them.

The camera connectors have been moved a bit inwards on the PCB, since placing them on the very edge would make it difficult to connect the screen.

Removed the inner layers of copper pours under the antenna to allow the signal not to be affected by copper pours. Finally decided to cut out that part of the PCB since the “[FR4 material itself will tune the antenna to lower frequencies](https://muehlhaus.com/support/antenna/esp32-antenna-pcbsize)” The final keep out is 15mm up and 5mm down due to space constraints. The downside cannot be 15mm
