Due to a DRC error in which the solder mask for the camera connector’s attachment pin was bridging different nets, I decided to delete the front mask polygon and draw one myself to try to resolve the issue, and it worked.
![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 12.13.25 PM.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwNDUsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--0dad359494d2dd70539c1f3158c404f840f2fed0/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 12.13.25 PM.png)
I had this error. My traces to the inductor are not actually in the keepout zone, but it still tells me they are not allowed. I decided to decrease the inductor’s keepout zone on both sides to resolve the DRC error, and it worked.

I decided to add a switch to turn the power on and off so that, for example, it can be switched off at night and the owner is not disturbed by the owner’s friend. There will probably be a software mode for this feature as well, but it is always nice to have a physical one that is accessible and easy to use.

A push button is also added to allow quicker control of the system’s features. While adding the push button, the footprint looked a little strange, but I managed to figure out how the switch worked.

When not pressed, 2 and 3, 6 and 5 are connected. When pressed, 1 and 2, 4 and 5 are connected.
![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 12.32.21 PM.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwNDYsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--081aca96adb355609f34243350224eb438c3be36/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 12.32.21 PM.png)
This switch can be added because we previously decided to remove the back screen, which gives me one more pin on the ESP32's GPIO 5.

While trying to connect the slide switch, I realized that the footprint did not match the component because the picture showed through-hole pins, but the footprint I got only had SMD pads. I then selected a slightly different model of the switch and obtained a footprint with through-hole pads.
![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 1.35.21 PM.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwNDgsInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--94e2cc7498e73f4e101e4ba32b52674f8a553805/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 1.35.21 PM.png)
When connecting this switch, I only updated the footprint, assuming the symbol was the same. The symbol’s pin numbers were actually different, so I looked for the symbol that comes with the footprint, but it didn’t include any connection details. I then checked the datasheet and found a diagram that made it clear it behaves the same as the other switch, just with different pin numbers. I edited the current symbol to fix the issue.
![Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 6.40.50 PM.png](/user-attachments/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6MTMwNDksInB1ciI6ImJsb2JfaWQifX0=--ca05a2aa5ac256fcf33daf2f029538de4ffdbda8/Screenshot 2026-05-08 at 6.40.50 PM.png)
When looking at polarity, the schematic priority is to ensure the schematics have two pins for the shell and that they are correctly labeled. The pin does not exist in your footprint because, in a footprint, pins are mechanical and therefore don’t have a pad number. Change them to through-hole so that I can match the schematics to the footprint. It is OK because most USB-C headers are so adorable.
